How a correlated parlay should be priced

independent:  P(A and B) = p(A) x p(B)                      decimal odds = 1 / P
correlated:   P(A and B) = P(Z1 > z1, Z2 > z2 ; rho)        bivariate normal
              with z_i = (line_i - projection_i) / sigma_i

For the Green Bay pair this is not a rounding error: pricing the two legs independently returns a joint probability of 0.2499 against 0.4292 with the measured correlation — the independent price is wrong by 71.72%.

Correlations are not stable. The Detroit pair (r = 0.2768, n = 6) shows how much a point estimate can move on a short sample. Any production pricing engine should re-estimate these on a rolling window and widen the interval rather than trusting a single number.
