A1Spread and total legs settle at -110 (decimal 1.9091).The mirror stores closing prices only for moneylines; the two-way spread/total market is conventionally priced at -110.All non-moneyline ROI numbers scale linearly with this price.material

Irregularities found during review

Discrepancies, documentation conflicts and data quirks
IDFindingDetailResolutionSource
I1nflverse `spread_line` sign convention disagrees with its own documentation.nflfastR documentation describes +7.5 as the home team being a 7.5-point underdog. In this file the opposite holds: over 108 completed 2026 games, corr(spread_line, home_moneyline) = -0.976 and corr(spread_line, home_margin) = +0.334, i.e. a positive value means the HOME team is favoured. The pipeline therefore uses the empirical convention and build_dataset.py fails loudly if the correlation ever flips.Empirical convention adopted and unit-guarded.source
I2Two different club abbreviations for Arizona.nfl.com prints 'AZ'; the mirror prints 'ARI'. An explicit alias map is applied before comparing (also LAR->LA, OHSH->WAS, JAC->JAX and relocations).Alias map in scripts/verify_official.py; no rows dropped.source
I3Weekly player/team statistics releases are unreachable, so the prop sample is tiny.Every nflverse-data release asset redirects to release-assets.githubusercontent.com and the TLS handshake is refused from this sandbox. Prop work therefore rests on six hand-captured nfl.com game logs (44 player-games) instead of a full season of game-level data.Prop strategies are published but blocked by gate G1 (sample) and marked 'needs review'.source
I4No player-prop prices anywhere in reach.Prop ROI uses the -110 assumption (A2). No edge is claimed for any prop.Flagged; prop rows carry no rank.—
I5NFL.com does not publish betting lines, so 'our line vs the market' compares against a third-party record of sportsbook closes.The market column comes from the nflverse mirror, not from the league.Labelled 'market (third-party)' everywhere it appears.source
I6NFL.com player-stats category pages lose their column headers in text extraction.Rows arrive as bare number sequences whose order cannot be proven, so publishing a 'receiving yards' value from those pages would be a guess. They are used only to pick players, never as a value source.Values come from each player's own page, where every column has a printed header.source
I7The mirror snapshot is mid-round for 2026 Week 8.108 of 272 scheduled 2026 regular-season games were complete at capture (through the Thursday Week 8 game). Week 8 is therefore excluded from the published board and only Weeks 9 and 10 are originated.Board limited to Weeks 9-10.source
I8Closing lines are single-sourced.One mirror, no independent sportsbook cross-check. A stale or mis-keyed price would propagate into every ROI figure.Accepted with disclosure; totals/spreads still reproduce every official game result.source
I9Game-page URLs verified only for a subset of fixtures.The page-fetch tool returned the Week 8 and Week 9 schedule pages in chunks; eight Week 9 game-page URLs were seen verbatim and are stored. Every other board row links to the official weekly schedule page instead of a constructed URL, so no dead link is published.Rows fall back to the verified weekly page.source
I10NFL rulebook is not cited.The rulebook URL this project would have used returns a 404 from nfl.com, and no rule text was independently verified, so no rule is quoted.Rules removed from the analysis entirely.source

Prop and parlay disclosures

  • Prop ROI uses a −110 assumption (A2); no prop price was reachable, so no prop strategy is ranked.
  • The prop sample is 41 player-games across 6 players, captured by hand from nfl.com. It is a demonstration of method, not a tested edge.
  • Same-game correlations rest on 14 paired games; the interval is wide.
  • Week 10 fixtures are from the mirror and not yet cross-checked (I9); those rows say so.
  • The closing line is single-sourced and is not a league publication (I8, I5).
