1. The one-paragraph version

We built our own NFL spread, total and moneyline from team ratings estimated walk-forward on 21 seasons of priced games, tested 27 pre-registered signals and 18 strategy families (singles, parlays, same-game combinations, teasers), and subject every one of them to seven validation gates. No strategy qualified. That is the finding, not a failure of the exercise: the data says the closing line is extremely well priced, that most apparent edges vanish once you correct for multiple testing, and that a strategy needs roughly 6,000 settled bets before a 2% edge is even visible. The project ships the machinery, the evidence, and a live dashboard instead of a fantasy bankroll.

4. Findings that matter

4.1 The closing line is beatable only at large model disagreements — and not provably at all. Our model's edge curve is monotone: at ≥3 points of disagreement with the close, 2,221 bets returned +2.8% with a 53.0% hit rate; at ≥4 points, 1,533 bets returned +3.8% with 53.6%. Both are the shape of a real signal and neither is significant (p = 0.27 and 0.17). The honest conclusion is "promising, unproven", and the sample needed to settle it is roughly four times what history provides.

4.2 Every popular public angle is already priced. Home underdogs −2.2%, divisional home dogs −4.3%, fading big home favourites −3.8%, rest advantages −4.5%, buying cold teams −4.2%, dome overs +0.5%. Twenty-seven signals, four of which looked interesting before correction, none of which survived. Two that looked best (low totals, cold-weather unders) actually flipped sign out of sample.

4.3 Parlays multiply the vig, not the edge. Two −110 legs need 27.4% of tickets to win to break even; two independent 52.4% legs win 27.4% of the time. Every parlay family tested returns less than the single bets inside it. The exception worth understanding is the same-game ticket, where correlation genuinely changes the fair price: a book that multiplies independent leg probabilities mis-prices any pair with |φ| meaningfully above zero.

4.4 Correlation values, measured not assumed. Home moneyline × home cover ATS: φ = 0.672 (95% CI 0.657–0.686). Spread × total in the same game: φ = 0.025 (CI −0.002 to 0.051) — effectively independent. QB passing yards over our line × team total over: φ = 0.198. RB rushing × team total: 0.073. Receiver yards × team total: 0.074. QB passing × at least one of his receivers (any-receiver event, not a pregame-chosen player): 0.157. Different games, same week: −0.005 (week-clustered 95% interval −0.014 to 0.005), so the classic parlay independence assumption is consistent with the data across games (a week-clustered check, not a proof of joint independence) and clearly violated inside a game.

4.5 Luck can be measured and mostly explains everything. Twenty thousand simulated zero-edge bettors placing 1,000 bets each at −110 produce a return standard deviation of 3.01%; the 95th percentile is +0.4% and the best of every hundred such bettors averages +3.1%. Team-season ATS dispersion does not exceed a permutation null that preserves the one-winner-per- game constraint (observed 0.01301 vs null 0.01521 ± 0.00085), team ATS split-half reliability is −0.058, and year-over-year reliability is −0.035. There is no detectable team-level ATS skill to exploit.

4.6 Teaser "edges" do not survive this data. The classic Wong construction (6-point teasers on +1.5/+2.5 dogs and −7.5/−8.5 favourites) covers 68.7% and 67.8% per leg after the six points in this sample, so a two-team ticket at −110 wins about 46% — a loss of 13.5% per ticket. Whatever edge existed in earlier eras is not present in 2006–2026 as recorded here.

4.7 Player props: we can originate a line, but not cash it. Our prop line is well calibrated out of sample (48.6%–50.9% over rates on 2024, against 42.6%–56.6% for the recency baseline), which is the thing that actually matters for setting a fair number. But there is no sportsbook prop price in any verified source, so a prop strategy cannot be settled against a market, cannot pass the significance or price-shock gates, and is published as unpriced research rather than as a leaderboard entry. This is the largest gap in the brief and it is flagged as F3.

8. Limitations in one place

Odds provenance is undocumented by the publisher (single source, no line shopping, no live prices). Player data covers two seasons and comes from a mirror. There is no injury, practice, roster or travel data, and the weather fields have no documented measurement protocol. League-official verification is a spot check, not an automated diff. Full detail: docs/flagged.html, docs/methods.html.
