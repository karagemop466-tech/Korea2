Player prop lines

Originating player prop numbers for four markets, graded out of sample on calibration — and why they cannot be cashed.

These lines are ours, not a market's. No sportsbook prop price exists in any verified source here, so a prop strategy cannot be settled against a market and cannot pass the significance or price-shock gates. The prop layer is published as unpriced research, excluded from the ranked leaderboard, and flagged as F3.

How the prop line is built

Three causal ingredients, each computed only from weeks before the game: a recency-weighted player average (weights 0.35/0.25/0.20/0.12/0.08 over the last five games), the player's own median residual against that average, and an opponent adjustment that is shrunk toward the league mean by how many games that defence has actually played. The third ingredient is the one that failed: opponent adjustment added noise to MAE in every market, which the table below shows honestly rather than hiding.

Calibration is the part that works

A line is only useful if half the outcomes land on each side of it. The plain recency average is systematically high for right-skewed markets (43–56% over rates); adding the player's own median residual pulls every market back to 48.6–50.9%. That is a real improvement in line quality — and it still does not make the line profitable, because there is no market to beat.
