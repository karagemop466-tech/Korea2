14 items that need a human decision: unverifiable values, assumptions, and places where two trusted sources disagree.

Nothing here blocks the release. These are the items a reviewer should look at first: values that could not be confirmed from a primary source, assumptions that had to be made, and places where two sources disagree. Each carries its sources, the effect on the published numbers, and the action that would close it. 14 items.

F1 · needs review

Odds provenance is undocumented by the publisher

What. spread_line, total_line, away_moneyline and home_moneyline in nflverse/nfldata carry no documented sportsbook, snapshot time, or open/close label. The publisher's own data dictionary describes the fields but not their source.

Why it matters. Every ROI in this project depends on those prices. If they are a single book's closing snapshot, the results are internally consistent but not reproducible against a specific sportsbook the reader can bet at.

Impact on published results. Directional: results are usable as a market benchmark, not as a live betting plan.

Sources. S1 · S2

Requested action. Ask the publisher / reconcile against a licensed odds feed.

F2 · needs review

Player-week data is a third-party mirror, two seasons only

What. The canonical nflverse player_stats release CDN is unreachable from the build shell, so 2023-2024 weekly player data comes from a third-party repository that uses the nflverse schema.

Why it matters. The prop layer therefore rests on 11,250 player-weeks, not the full history.

Impact on published results. Limits the player-prop conclusions to two seasons; no season-to-season stability test.

Sources. S9 · S8

Requested action. Re-run scripts/build_props.py with the canonical release.

F3 · needs review

No sportsbook player-prop prices exist in any verified source

What. The extracts contain game-level spread/total/moneyline prices only. There is no prop line, price, or settled prop market anywhere in the data.

Why it matters. A prop strategy cannot be settled against a market, so it cannot pass the significance or price-shock gates. Prop entries are published as an unpriced research tier and are excluded from the ranked leaderboard.

Impact on published results. A9/A10 (prop combos) are met in research terms only.

Sources. S9

Requested action. Supply a licensed prop-odds history to price these strategies.

F4 · needs review

Teaser price is an assumption, not a sourced number

What. The 2-team 6-point teaser is settled at −110, the conventional price. No teaser price exists in the verified extracts.

Why it matters. A teaser's profitability is extremely price-sensitive (−110 vs −120).

Impact on published results. The teaser entry is reported with the assumption exposed; at −110 it loses 13.5% per ticket, so no plausible price rescues it.

Sources. S1

Requested action. Replace the assumption with a sourced teaser price.

F5 · needs review

Parlay push settlement is conservative and provider-specific

What. When one leg of a parlay pushes and the others win, this project settles the whole ticket as a push. Sportsbooks usually reduce the ticket instead.

Why it matters. The convention understates parlay returns, never overstates them.

Impact on published results. Parlay ROIs are lower bounds.

Sources. S1

Requested action. Adopt the settlement rule of the book you actually bet.

F6 · needs review

Opening lines exist for the 2021 season only

What. initial_lines.csv covers 2021 weeks 1-18 and nothing else.

Why it matters. Closing-line-value analysis needs opening prices; a single season is too small.

Impact on published results. The line-movement probe (S13) is exploratory and fails the sample gate by design.

Sources. S3

Requested action. Source a multi-season opening-line history.

F7 · needs review

League-official numeric verification is a spot check, not automated

What. nfl.com is blocked from the build shell. League-official pages were read with the agent's page-fetch tool and compared by hand: (a) the 2026 NFL.com standings matched the nflverse standings file on 24 of 24 published teams (W-L-T, points for, points against); (b) the 2026 week-8 final Carolina at Green Bay is 19-27 on the league scores page and 19-27 in the game file; (c) 14 further 2026 win-loss records matched.

Why it matters. Everything else is reconciled between two nflverse extracts, not against the league.

Impact on published results. No numeric claim here is contradicted by a league source, but most are not league-verified.

Sources. S10 · S6

Requested action. Run a scheduled diff against NFL.com with network access.

F9 · needs review

The two closing-line extracts disagree on 8.7% of game-sides

What. Reconciling games.csv against closing_lines.csv over 2006-2018: 2,095 of 3,096 home-side lines match exactly, 91.3% agree within 0.5 pt, 97.2% within 1 pt, and the largest gap is 5.5 pts.

Why it matters. Sub-point gaps are plausible (different books or snapshots); large gaps are not.

Impact on published results. Settlement uses games.csv throughout; the alternative extract is not used for ROI.

Sources. S1 · S2

Requested action. Resolve the residual gaps against a licensed feed.

Impact on published results. Affects 2016-2020 ratings for three franchises only.

F13 · needs review

Why it matters. The wind-under signal is the strongest entry on the board and it depends on this field.

Impact on published results. If wind is measured at a different time/place than kickoff, the signal weakens.

Values deliberately left blank

The following are blank on the site rather than estimated:

  • Market lines for the 136 unplayed games without a recorded price — see the live slate tables on the Originated lines page.
  • Player-prop market prices — do not exist in any verified source, so no prop ROI is published (F3).
  • Teaser and parlay payout multipliers — provider-specific and not in the data; the teaser is priced at a stated assumption and the parlay push rule is stated explicitly (F4, F5).
  • Closing-line-value figures beyond 2021 — opening prices exist for one season only (F6).
