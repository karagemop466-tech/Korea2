EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

What this review found,
and what it changes.

A one-page summary of the Decision Desk: the state of the project, the numbers that survived an independent check, the ones that did not, the missing inputs that block a decision, and the order in which to fix them.

STATE

1. Where the project stands

The repository is an unusually well-documented research effort that has reached an honest negative result. The closing market beats the model (9.711 vs 10.391 mean absolute error on 936 walk-forward games), the model adds nothing after controlling for the close (coefficient -0.019, p = 0.881), and 23 candidate signals tested in this session all fail the validation gates. None of that is a failure of the work; it is the work doing its job.

What is not settled is anything that depends on timing. There is one un-timestamped closing line per game and no committed lineup feed, so the point-in-time gate cannot pass for any strategy, and the injury and backup-depth question cannot be answered from this repository at all.

SEQUENCE

5. The recommended sequence

  1. Fund nothing.

    No operator, jurisdiction, licence or limit has been verified, and no prize pool exists. Keep every reward tier unfunded.

  2. Capture timestamped prices.

    Ten days of that is worth more than another model, because it is the only route to closing-line value and to passing gate G1.

  3. Commit the injury, depth and snap feeds on a runner.

    They exist upstream under CC BY 4.0; they are missing here, not missing from the world.

  4. Fix the two statistics that are wrong.

    The ATS coin-flip test is 50% by construction, and the README's model MAE is stale against the data.

  5. Then measure what a starting quarterback is worth.

    It is the one genuinely new signal with a plausible path to clearing G1.

FALSIFIABILITY

6. What would change this conclusion

  • A timestamped price series that shows a repeatable gap between a captured price and the close.
  • A depth-chart and snap-count history that shows a measurable, market-inefficient effect from losing a specific starter.
  • A licensed prop price history that turns the measured correlation gap into a testable, priced edge.
  • A candidate that passes all ten gates out of sample, with a positive lower bound and a luck test it survives.

Absent one of those four, the correct decision is to keep researching and to fund nothing. That is the recommendation this summary makes.
