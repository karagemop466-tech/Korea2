Standings, rewards and the rule that binds them.

LEADERBOARD

Ranked by evidence, not by record.

Rank by SIGNAL ONLY: gates passed, then Benjamini-Hochberg-adjusted p (lower = stronger skill evidence), then sample size. Staking metrics (ROI and its CI) are reported under `staking` but never affect rank ordering, so a lucky staking run cannot mask a bad signal. A strategy can only earn a reward when all ten gates pass; a positive ROI alone earns nothing.

0 strategies are eligible for a reward. Ranking still happens, because a rejected strategy's ranking tells you which hypothesis is least wrong and therefore worth refining first.

Signal vs staking

Signal is the strategy's evidence edge: whether it clears all ten validation gates (data integrity, out-of-sample holdout, significance vs break-even, sample size, CLV, multiple-comparison control, correlation screen, execution, reproducibility). Staking is what the sizing actually earns or loses (ROI, bankroll drawdown). The leaderboard is ranked by signal only — gates passed, then Benjamini-Hochberg-adjusted p, then sample size — and only a 10/10 gate score is eligible for capital, so a positive ROI from luck alone is never rewarded and a lucky staking run cannot mask a bad signal. A live strategy whose signal later decays is returned to paper by the demotion gate (register row G10; code-enforced as D1 via evaluate_demotion()).

How the ranking is computed

  1. Every registered strategy is settled on the same out-of-sample window.
  2. Each is scored against the ten gates, with the binomial p-value corrected for the number of tests.
  3. Survivors are ranked by gates passed, then by BH-adjusted p; ties break on sample size (signal only — ROI is reported under a separate staking group and never affects ordering).
  4. Only a 10/10 score is eligible for capital. Anything less stays in the research record. A live strategy that later decays is returned to paper by the demotion gate (register row G10; code-enforced as D1 via evaluate_demotion()).
