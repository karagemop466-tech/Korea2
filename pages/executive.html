EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

What we found, in one page.

For a reader who wants the conclusion, the evidence behind it, and the exact places where the evidence runs out.

1. The decision

Do not allocate to any strategy in this build. 7 candidate strategies were defined and tested on 2023-2026 games, settled at the closing line. Zero passed the ten validation gates. The highest-ranked of them returned -1.2% per unit staked, which is roughly the cost of the vig.

That is the intended output of a gate system. A framework that approves everything on its first attempt is not measuring anything.

3. Our own lines are worse, and we can prove it

The originated lines come from a transparent weighted point-ratings model: two equations per game, recency-weighted, ridge-shrunk, with the home-field edge shrunk toward a ten-season prior. It is honest work and it is not good enough. Out-of-sample spread MAE is 10.39 points against 9.71 for the closing line.

The decisive test is not accuracy but whether the model knows anything the closing line does not. Regressing the actual margin on both gives a model coefficient of -0.019 (HC1 p = 0.881) for spreads and -0.149 (p = 0.247) for totals. A coefficient indistinguishable from zero means the market has already absorbed what the model knows.

The wider the disagreement, the worse the model looks: disagreement is model error, not edge.

4. Correlation is where repeatable structure lives

Four same-team legs taken at their own medians land together 11.1% of the time, not the 5.7% that multiplication implies (n = 7560). The strongest measured relationships sit inside a team's passing game (QB to WR1 r = 0.562), and the clearest negative pair is rushing against passing (r = -0.127), which is game script. These relationships are stable and physically explicable, and they are the part of this project that is useful independently of forecasting skill: they tell you what a parlay is really worth.

5. Luck, removed

Pythagorean expectation explains 81.9% of season win-rate variance across 877 team-seasons. The residual - the luck - has a standard deviation of 1.32 wins and a year-over-year correlation of 0.024: past luck does not predict future luck. Any strategy that ranks teams on win-loss record is ranking them partly on noise.

7. Player props: measured, but not priceable here

Prop lines are originated from usage history and graded on 22,892 player-games from 2023 onward. The model beats a season-to-date mean by between -0.35% and 0.84% depending on the stat, and the originated lines are calibrated: the over rate at the line is close to 50%.

No ROI is published. The official feed contains no historical player-prop prices, so there is no honest way to compute one. Inventing a price would be the easiest way to manufacture a fake edge, so this is flagged rather than filled in.

8. What would change the conclusion

  • A strategy that beats the closing line out of sample on 1,000+ settled bets with a bootstrap ROI lower bound above zero, surviving Benjamini-Hochberg across every test tried.
  • Verified executable prices with timestamps, so closing-line value can be measured instead of assumed.
  • A historical player-prop price archive, which would turn the prop model from a measurement into a testable strategy.
  • Confirmation of the NFL injury-report policy text from an official source, which would unblock injury-based signals.
