Parlays and combos

Parlays are the hardest place to hide a bad edge, because a 3-leg ticket needs three winners and the correlation between legs decides whether the price is fair. 7 parlay and moneyline strategies were built; together they graded 7,594 tickets and none cleared the gates.

Are the legs independent?

Measured on realised leg outcomes in the held-out window. "Independent implied" is the product of the legs' de-vigged probabilities, i.e. what a book quotes when it ignores correlation.

Cross-game parlays are close to independent — the gaps are within a fraction of a point, which is expected when the legs come from different games. A same-game parlay is a different animal: our SGP (spread + total, same game) actually lands -1.24 pp below its independent price, because those two legs are very slightly anti-correlated in practice. The effect is small, but it is systematic and it points the other way from the usual 'SGPs are correlated, so they are worse value' claim for this particular pair of markets.

What correlation does to a same-game price

A two-leg same-game parlay at standard −110/−110 would be quoted at +300 by a book that assumed independence. Here is the empirical joint probability from 7,080 games, and the fair price implied by it.

The error runs from -4.6% to 2.7% depending on the side combination (a negative error means the independent quote under-prices the combo, i.e. it favours the bettor). At these magnitudes correlation is not where the money is lost — the vig is.

Chain pricing, end to end

For each parlay ticket we multiply the legs' closing decimal prices, settle all legs, and stake one unit. A single losing leg loses the stake; a push voids the stake. That is standard parlay arithmetic, and it is what the ROI column measures.
