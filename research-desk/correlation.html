How to estimate, when evidence exists

  1. Collect official, paired, game-level outcomes; document population, season, participation, timestamps and exclusions. Missing is not zero.
  2. For continuous outcomes compute Pearson r = covariance(X,Y) / (sd(X) × sd(Y)); for binary outcomes use the same formula on 0/1 indicators. If a variance is zero, report undefined, not zero.
  3. Estimate only on training-period data; use game-block bootstrap intervals and report n, time period and selection criteria. Compare with holdout joint-event calibration and score, not correlation alone.
  4. For two binary legs, P(A and B) = pA × pB + r × √[pA(1−pA)pB(1−pB)]. This identity requires a valid correlation consistent with the marginals; it does not generalize to pricing all higher-order combinations from pairwise correlations alone.

Correlation is not causation, and historic fit does not ensure good forecasts. Read NIST caution ↗

!
Joint pricing requires more than a correlation matrix.

For three or more legs, estimate and test a coherent multivariate joint distribution. Evaluate tail probabilities, participation, pushes, and bookmaker rules explicitly.
