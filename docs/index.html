What this project found

1 · The market is very hard to beat, and we can prove it

Our walk-forward rating model lands within 10.69 points of the actual margin; the closing line lands within 10.26. Correlation between the two is 0.769. Betting every model disagreement loses about 2.5% per bet — the vig plus model error.

2 · Large disagreements are the only place an edge appears

When the model disagrees with the close by 4+ points, the hit rate rises to 53.6% and the return to +3.8% per bet over 1,533 bets — still not significant (p = 0.17). The monotone pattern is informative; the sample is not yet large enough.

3 · Zero of 18 strategies qualify

Nothing passes all seven gates. The closest entry, wind unders, returns +8.1% out of sample over 449 bets but reaches p = 0.12 and fails the multiple-testing and drawdown gates. See Validation gates.

4 · Same-game legs are correlated; cross-game legs are not

Home moneyline × home cover: φ = 0.672. QB passing yards over × team total over: φ = 0.198. Different games in the same week: φ = 0.029, i.e. the standard parlay independence assumption holds only across games.

5 · Team-level ATS "skill" is not detectable

A permutation null that reassigns ATS winners inside each game produces at least as much dispersion as the real data. Split-half reliability of team ATS records is -0.058. See Luck removal.

6 · What it takes to prove an edge

A true 2% return needs roughly 6,156 settled bets before it is distinguishable from noise at 95% confidence — about 41 seasons at 150 bets a year. That is why gate G1 exists.

How to read this site

Every table row carries a source id that links to a registry entry with the URL, HTTP status, row count and SHA-256 of the file it came from. Where a value could not be confirmed, the cell is blank and the row is published on the Flagged for review page with an impact assessment. Percentages in green are positive, red negative; returns are per 1-unit stake, never compounded, always settled at the price recorded in the source.
