Validation gates

Seven mechanical gates, applied identically to every strategy, with the full pass/fail matrix published.

The gates are the product. Anyone can produce a backtest that shows +8%. The gates are what distinguish a result from a coincidence: a sample big enough to matter, a vig-aware significance test, a second untouched window, a price haircut, a drawdown ceiling, a best-of-N correction, and a minimum betting frequency. All seven, or the strategy is unranked.

Gate definitions

The seven validation gates
GateRequirement
G1_samplen >= 150 settled bets in the validation window
G2_significancefull-sample p < 0.05 vs the vig-aware break-even AND Benjamini-Hochberg q < 0.10 across the whole programme (27 signals + 18 strategies)
G3_consistencydiscovery ROI > 0, validation ROI > 0, >= 55% of validation seasons positive
G4_price_shockvalidation ROI stays > 0 when every recorded price is 5 cents worse
G5_drawdownvalidation max drawdown <= 20u and no season > 40% of validation profit
G6_reality_checkmax-statistic block-bootstrap p < 0.10 (best-of-N correction)
G7_coverage>= 25 bets per season on average in the validation window

What it would take to qualify

A 2% edge needs about 6,156 settled bets to reach one-sided 95% confidence, and about 14,048 for 80% power. The best strategy on the board has 449 bets. That mismatch, not bad luck, is why the cycle paid out nothing.

The two gates that do the work

  • G2 — significance vs the price. Not against 50%, against the break-even rate implied by the prices actually taken, then corrected for the size of the research programme.
  • G6 — best-of-N. A block bootstrap over weeks gives the distribution of the best strategy's return when nothing is skilful. If a strategy's return sits inside that distribution, it is scenery.
