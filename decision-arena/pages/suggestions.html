SUG-01 · Ingest a timestamped odds archive before any opening/closing claim is made

Evidence. The schedules feed stores ONE price per market and no sampling time, so 7,103 of 7,103 snapshots are graded `unattributed_single_observation`. Only 2021 has a separate early snapshot, and the publisher does not document or date it.

Proposal. Add a fetch step for a source that publishes an explicit timestamp per price (open and close), then re-grade every snapshot. Until then the price page keeps the `unattributed` grade and the opening-number study stays limited to n=67.

Impact. HIGH - without it, line-move signals and closing-line value cannot be measured at all.
Cost. Medium: one new feed, one schema change, one re-run. Licensing may cost money.
Risk. A cheap feed can be a stale or unofficial one; grading it `verified` would be worse than the current honest blank.
Unblocks. DA-S01 (market-move fade), per-bet CLV, the demotion rule DM-1, and any claim about beating the close.

Blocking question Q-01: Where can a timestamped opening and closing price be obtained, for which seasons and markets, and under what licence?

SUG-02 · Refresh the results feed in-season, weekly, and diff it against NFL.com

Evidence. The committed snapshot is frozen at gameday 2026-11-01. The official Week 10 Thursday game (WAS 24, NYG 21) is published on NFL.com but absent from the snapshot, so the current slate cannot be scored from the committed data.

Proposal. Run the existing build-verify/data-pipeline schedule, then have this hub re-fetch the schedules feed and re-reconcile the most recent completed week against the official schedule page, publishing a per-week diff count.

Impact. HIGH - a stale results feed makes every backtest and ROI figure describe history, not the current season.
Cost. Low: the workflow already runs; this adds one reconcile step.
Risk. Official pages are hand-read here; an automated fetch of nfl.com may be blocked, in which case the diff must stay manual and be labelled so.

SUG-03 · Stop publishing the rating model as a market competitor; publish it as a signal generator

Evidence. Walk-forward, the model's side error is worse than the market's in every evaluated season (2023: 10.65 vs 9.90 pts; 2024: 11.18 vs 9.61; 2025: 11.13 vs 9.72; 2026: 9.92 vs 9.48), and its ATS win rate on its own side is 47.8% / 48.9% / 43.8% / 45.0%.

Proposal. Keep the fair/market/difference columns (they are the useful output) but label the model explicitly as a benchmark-relative signal generator, not a line-beating model. Remove any 'we originate better lines' framing that the data does not support.

Impact. MEDIUM - protects the reader from acting on an overstatement; costs nothing statistically.
Cost. Low: wording and one verdict field.
Risk. Under-selling: a future model could beat the market, and the label must then be re-earned by the same walk-forward test.
Unblocks. A clean basis for the leaderboard's benchmark column.

Blocking question Q-03: Is the rating model meant to beat the closing line, or only to describe a team?

SUG-04 · Add a price-aware edge filter so 'difference' becomes an actionable number

Evidence. The difference column currently compares the model's fair line to the market's number. Without a price per side, a 3-point difference on a -115 price is not the same bet as on -105, and 1,584 of DA-S08's 5,909 opportunities carry no price at all.

Proposal. Publish, per originated game, the break-even price implied by the model's probability, next to the actual price where one exists, and mark the bet 'positive' only when the price clears break-even.

Impact. HIGH - converts a descriptive difference into a decision rule with a cost.
Cost. Low: arithmetic only, using prices already present.
Risk. A positive-EV number computed from an uncalibrated model will look more authoritative than it is; it must carry the model's own error bar.
Unblocks. A defensible staking rule and the price-discipline gate DA-G7.

Blocking question Q-04: What price is actually available to the account, and is it the same price the model is scored against?

SUG-06 · Define a 'depth lost' index before measuring injury impact

Evidence. The official report lists 66 players Out across 28 clubs for Week 10, but carries no depth rank, so 'the starter is out' and 'the 4th string is out' are indistinguishable in the official data.

Proposal. Pre-commit a formula: depth_lost = sum over Out/Doubtful players of 1/(depth_rank), by side of ball, and publish it per club-week next to the raw report. Fix the formula before looking at any outcome variable.

Impact. MEDIUM - makes injury impact comparable across clubs and testable.
Cost. Low once depth ranks exist (SUG-05).
Risk. Any weighting is a choice; publishing the formula first prevents choosing it after seeing results.
Unblocks. An injury-and-depth signal family, and the user's starter/backup requirement.

Blocking question Q-06: What weighting makes 'depth lost' comparable across positions and clubs?

SUG-07 · Price prop combos only after a prop-price feed exists; do not back-fit them

Evidence. Receptions vs receiving yards correlate at r = 0.80 (n = 45,315) and QB yards vs WR1 at 0.55 (n = 5,893), but no committed feed holds a historical player-prop price, so DA-S10/DA-S11 have n = 0 priced bets and are EXECUTION_BLOCKED.

Proposal. Keep the measured correlations published. When a prop-price archive exists, price the combos at the measured joint rate and run them through the same ten gates, at the same sample floor as singles.

Impact. MEDIUM - unlocks a whole family without inventing results now.
Cost. High: prop price archives are expensive or unavailable.
Risk. Prop prices move fastest and are most often limited; a backtest on quoted prices may not be executable at those stakes.
Unblocks. DA-S10, DA-S11, and the prop half of the competition.

Blocking question Q-07: Is there any prop-price history available at all, and at what cost?

SUG-08 · Raise the sample window instead of lowering the gate

Evidence. Every strategy fails gate DA-G2 (n >= 300 graded and n >= 100 priced). The best-populated family is DA-S08 with 4,325 graded and 2,741 priced; the worst is DA-S12 with 157. Nine seasons of prices exist at most, and only 73.2% of spread legs carry one.

Proposal. Do not weaken DA-G2. Instead extend the priced history (more seasons with prices, SUG-01) and pool variants that are pre-specified as one family rather than counted as separate tests.

Impact. MEDIUM - the difference between 'untested' and 'tested'.
Cost. Low (uses data already held).
Risk. Pooling variants raises power but hides heterogeneity; the pooled rule must be registered before scoring.
Unblocks. Any strategy reaching PAPER status.

Blocking question Q-08: Would you rather have a lower statistical bar or a longer wait?

SUG-10 · Wire the demotion gate to an automatic weekly evaluation

Evidence. Six demotion triggers (DM-1..DM-6) and two promotion rules are defined and published, but nothing currently evaluates them on a schedule; with 0 live strategies the rules are untested in operation.

Proposal. Run the demotion evaluation in the same weekly job as the results refresh, write the state transitions to a log, and publish 'stale' when a strategy stops receiving prices (DM-6).

Impact. MEDIUM - the difference between a rule and a ritual.
Cost. Low.
Risk. An automated demotion on a thin sample could churn; the minimum-bet floors prevent that and must be enforced in code.
Unblocks. The user's 'define a demotion gate' requirement becoming operational rather than documentary.
