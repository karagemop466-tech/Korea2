2. The title/body week mismatch is not fixed

Both injury-report URLs return the document title “Official NFL Injury Report for Players - Week 11 of the 2026 Season” at this read, while NFL.com prints Injuries - WEEK 10 in its body with Week 10 fixtures, and NFL.com prints Injuries - WEEK 11. Earlier sessions flagged this; it is still live. Practical consequence, which is the part that matters: a scraper that pins to the document title will read the wrong week. Any capture job must pin to the body heading and to the fixtures, which is what this desk's verifier checks.

4. The game-status column is per-fixture, not per-week

At 2026-11-19T19:54:00Z exactly one fixture — IND at HOU, the Thursday night game — had a populated game-status column. The other twelve published practice status only. This single fact explains most of the noise in any naive week-over-week comparison:

  • 15 of 253 rows (6%) carry a designation at this read.
  • The Week 10 register, read after that week's games were designated, carries 171 of 347.
  • A raw comparison would therefore show ~150 players “losing” their designation — a 91% drop in one week with no football explanation whatsoever.

This is the finding with the most modelling consequence. Any feature built on “has a game designation” is a clock, not a player state, unless it is timestamped against the fixture's own designation deadline. The desk's delta encodes that rule explicitly: game status is compared for 2 clubs, practice status only for the rest.

5. Injury descriptions and designations travel together

15 rows carry an injury description (“Calf”, “Concussion”, “Foot, Illness”) and every one of them also carries a game designation; no row with a designation lacks an injury string. This is a useful structural invariant for a parser — and it is now an enforced build check (verify.py R5), not a hope.

6. What these findings do not license

  • They do not support a “backup deficiency” feature. The official pages used here publish no depth rank, no starter designation and no snap share, so a second- or third-string replacement cannot be identified from them. That requirement stays blocked on a source, exactly as FL-15/FL-19 record.
  • They do not support pricing anything. Two reads of one page, one week apart, cannot estimate the effect of an absence on a score. No ablation is possible without outcomes linked to these snapshots, and no outcomes are linked yet.
  • They do not show the Week 10 register was wrong. That register was a correct read of a page that had fully designated its week. The two reads are of the same page at different points in the league's weekly cycle.
