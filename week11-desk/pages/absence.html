Not listed ≠ available

The owner brief is explicit: “Capture the reserve list so 'not listed' never means 'available'”, and “Malik Willis is absent from the weekly report because he is on injured reserve, not because he is healthy.” This page is the concrete check.

The mechanism. The weekly injury report lists players with a practice or game designation. It does not list players on injured reserve, and it prints no “reason for absence” column. So a player missing from the table is unaccounted for, not cleared. This desk can therefore only publish two things: (1) that a named player is absent from the Week 11 table, and (2) the official nfl.com headline the report page itself links, which is the only published explanation available. It does not convert either into an availability flag.

Two rows that make the point

Lamar Jackson (BAL) appears nowhere in the Ravens' Week 11 table. Neither does a reason. A reader who treats “absent from the injury report” as “available” would have to conclude Baltimore's starting quarterback is fine — while an official nfl.com headline linked from the very same page reads “Ravens confirm Lamar Jackson expected to miss 6-10 weeks”. The report cannot tell you which. That is why this desk leaves availability blank.

Malik Willis (MIA) is likewise absent from the Dolphins' table, and the page links “Dolphins place QB Malik Willis (groin) on injured reserve”. Absent-from-table and healthy are different things.
